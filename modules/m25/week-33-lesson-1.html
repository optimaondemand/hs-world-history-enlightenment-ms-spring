TODAY'S FOCUS

In Europe the Cold War stayed cold, because neither side dared to cross the line between the two blocs. In Asia the line was less clear, and men fought and died over it. This lesson follows three struggles: the Communist victory in China, the war in Korea, and the long war in Vietnam. Ask in each case who made the decisive choices and what else might have happened.

CHINA: THE COMMUNISTS WIN

Read more below., held the cities and the government. The Communists, led by Mao Zedong, held the northern countryside. Civil war began, and in two years the tide turned. Chiang’s government was corrupt, and in 1948 prices rose so fast that wages and savings were wiped out. His armies lost the northeast, and whole units went over to the Communists with their American weapons. The Communists, meanwhile, won the peasants with promises of land. Beijing fell in January 1949.

On October 1, 1949, Mao stood in Tiananmen Square and proclaimed the People’s Republic of China. Chiang withdrew to the island of Taiwan with about two million followers. The loss of China shocked Americans. Republicans charged that Truman had “lost China,” and the charge troubled his party for years. Most historians answer that America never had the power to decide the war, and that Chiang lost it mainly through his own failures. In February 1950 Mao signed a treaty of alliance with Stalin. 👤 HISTORICAL FIGURE
Chiang Kai-shek
CHAHNG KY-SHEK

Chiang Kai-shek (1887 to 1975) trained as a soldier in Japan and rose to lead the Kuomintang after the death of its founder, Sun Yat-sen. In the late 1920s he united much of China under one government and drove the Communists from their bases. Later, during the war with Japan, his armies held out for eight years, at a cost of millions of lives.

After the Second World War his government was weakened by inflation, corruption, and the loss of the peasants’ trust. In 1949 he fled to Taiwan, where he ruled as president until his death. He kept the claim that his government was the true government of all China, and the United States defended that claim for decades.

KOREA: THE FIRST HOT WAR

Korea had been a Japanese colony. In 1945 American and Soviet officers agreed to divide it at the 38th parallel so that each could accept the Japanese surrender in its half. The line was meant to be temporary. By 1948 it had become a border between two governments: the Republic of Korea in the south, under Syngman Rhee, and the Communist North, under Kim Il Sung. Each claimed the whole country.

On June 25, 1950, North Korean tanks crossed the line. Kim had asked for Stalin’s approval, and Stalin gave it after Mao agreed to help if needed. Seoul fell in three days. Truman did not wait for Congress. He ordered American forces to Korea, and he took the matter to the United Nations. The Security Council voted to defend South Korea. The Soviet Union could have used its veto to block the vote, but its delegate was away, boycotting the Council over the seating of China. The American-led force was nearly pushed into the sea at Pusan. Then General Douglas MacArthurDUG-lus mak-AR-thurDouglas MacArthur was the American general who commanded United Nations forces in Korea until Truman removed him in 1951. Read more below. landed troops far behind the enemy at Inchon in September and cut the North Korean army in two.

At that point the aim changed. Instead of restoring the border, the United Nations force drove north toward the Yalu River, on the Chinese frontier. Mao warned that he would not accept an enemy army on his border. When the warning was ignored, hundreds of thousands of Chinese soldiers crossed the river in the bitter cold of November 1950 and drove the army back south of Seoul. The fighting settled near the 38th parallel and stayed there for two years. MacArthur wanted to bomb China and said that there was no substitute for victory. Truman refused. 👤 HISTORICAL FIGURE
Douglas MacArthur
DUG-lus mak-AR-thur

Douglas MacArthur (1880 to 1964) graduated first in his class at West Point and became a general in the First World War. In the Second World War he commanded in the Pacific, and in 1945 he accepted the Japanese surrender. As the ruler of occupied Japan he helped to build a democratic government there.

His landing at Inchon in 1950 was a bold stroke that many officers thought would fail. It succeeded, but he then misjudged China’s reaction and clashed with Truman over the aims of the war. Truman dismissed him on April 11, 1951. MacArthur was proud and gifted, and his ambition and certainty brought both his greatest success and his fall.

The United States saw the war as part of the contest with communism, and by 1954 it was paying most of France’s costs.

The war ended at a place called Dien Bien PhuDien Bien PhuDien Bien Phu was the valley fortress in northwest Vietnam where the Viet Minh defeated the French in 1954. A conference at Geneva then divided Vietnam at the 17th parallel, meant only as a pause. The Communists took the North, and elections to unite the country were set for 1956. They were never held. South Vietnam, under Ngo Dinh Diem, refused them, and the United States backed him. President Eisenhower had said that if Vietnam fell, the other countries of Southeast Asia would follow, like a row of dominoes. The United States sent money and advisers.

By the early 1960s Communist guerrillas, called the Viet Congvee-ET KONGA name used for the Communist guerrillas who fought the South Vietnamese government. In 1963 Diem was overthrown and killed in a coup that Washington had let happen. In August 1964 North Vietnamese boats attacked an American destroyer in the Gulf of Tonkin. A second attack was reported two days later, but historians now think that it never took place. Congress voted almost unanimously to let President Johnson use force as he saw fit. In 1965 he began regular bombing of the North and sent in combat troops. By 1968 more than half a million Americans were in Vietnam.

It was another proxy warPROK-see WORA war in which great powers fight each other by arming and aiding smaller nations or groups. See the glossary box., and a harder one than Korea. The enemy melted into the villages, and body counts did not prove that anyone was winning. In January 1968 the Communists struck at cities all over the South during the Tet holiday. They lost thousands of fighters and gained no city for long, but the attack shocked Americans who had been told that the war was nearly won. Johnson chose not to run again. President Nixon withdrew American troops step by step while the South Vietnamese army took over the fighting. A peace agreement was signed in Paris in January 1973, and the last American troops left in March. In April 1975 the North overran the South, and Saigon fell. About 58,000 Americans died, and well over a million Vietnamese. Laos and Cambodia also fell to Communist governments. The other nations of Southeast Asia did not fall, and historians still argue over whether that was because of the American stand.

👤 HISTORICAL FIGURE
Ho Chi Minh
HOH CHEE MIN

Ho Chi Minh (1890 to 1969) left Vietnam as a young man and worked on ships and in kitchens in Europe and America. In France after the First World War he became a Communist, and he spent many years abroad as an organizer. He founded the Viet Minh in 1941.

He was both a nationalist and a Communist, and he used the first to win followers for the second. In 1945 he borrowed words from the American Declaration of Independence to announce his country’s freedom. He ruled North Vietnam until his death in 1969, six years before his armies won the war. His government also jailed and killed many of its own people. Saigon was renamed Ho Chi Minh City in 1976.

🛡 BATTLE
Dien Bien Phu

The French chose the valley as a base to draw the Viet Minh into open battle, where French guns and planes would crush them. The valley was about ten miles long, ringed by hills, and supplied only by air. French commanders believed that the enemy could not bring heavy artillery over the mountains.

General Giap brought thousands of laborers, who dragged guns apart and hauled them into the hills by hand. The siege began on March 13, 1954. The Viet Minh guns closed the airstrip, and the fortress was overrun on May 7 after weeks of trench warfare. About ten thousand French troops were taken prisoner. France soon left Vietnam.

WHAT THE THREE WARS SHARE

Look back over the three wars. In each, a Communist force fought a government backed by the West. In each, the United States feared that a loss would encourage others, and in each, great powers stayed out of direct combat with one another.
