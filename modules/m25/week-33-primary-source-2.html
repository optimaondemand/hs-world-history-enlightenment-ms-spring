PRIMARY SOURCE 2
Khrushchev to Kennedy (October 26, 1962)
The Soviet leader writes privately to the president and offers a way out.
💡 BEFORE YOU READ

Khrushchev wrote this letter on the evening of October 26, and it reached Washington as night fell. It was long, personal, and written in haste, and some experts think that Khrushchev wrote it himself, without his advisers. A second and harder letter arrived the next day. Kennedy chose to answer the first.

The letter comes to us in an English translation made by the United States government at the time. A few words may be new. “Thermonuclear” refers to the hydrogen bomb. “Armaments” means weapons. “Catastrophe” means a great disaster. “Pledge” means a solemn promise.

Read this as a historian should. Ask what image Khrushchev uses for the crisis. Ask what he offers, and what he asks in return. Ask how the tone of this letter differs from the tone of Kennedy’s speech.

Mr. President, we and you ought not now to pull on the ends of the rope in which you have tied the knot of war, because the more the two of us pull, the tighter that knot will be tied. And a moment may come when that knot will be tied so tight that even he who tied it will not have the strength to untie it, and then it will be necessary to cut that knot.

[...]

If there is no intention to doom the world to the catastrophe of thermonuclear war, then let us not only relax the forces pulling on the ends of the rope, let us take measures to untie that knot. We are ready for this.

[...]

We, for our part, will declare that our ships, bound for Cuba, will not carry any kind of armaments. You would declare that the United States will not invade Cuba with its forces and will not support any sort of forces which might intend to invade Cuba. Then the necessity for the presence of our military specialists in Cuba would disappear.

📌 A NOTE FOR YOU

Notice that Khrushchev does not mention the American missiles in Turkey here; that demand came in the second letter. Ask why he led with the offer on Cuba alone, and what the rope and knot suggest about how he saw the two leaders. You can return to this page as often as you like.
