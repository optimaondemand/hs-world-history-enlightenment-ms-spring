PRIMARY SOURCE 1
Kennedy, Address on the Cuban Missile Crisis (October 22, 1962)
The president tells the nation about the missiles and announces the quarantine.
💡 BEFORE YOU READ

On the evening of October 22, 1962, President Kennedy spoke on television and radio to the American people. His advisers had argued for a week about what to say. About a hundred million people watched or listened. For most of them it was the first they had heard of the missiles. The speech was also read in Moscow, and Khrushchev heard it within hours.

A few words may be new. “Quarantine” means a ring of ships that stops vessels bound for a certain place. “Retaliatory” means done in return for an attack. “Hemisphere” means half of the world; the Western Hemisphere includes North and South America. “Surveillance” means close watching. “Clandestine” means secret.

Read this as a historian should. Ask what Kennedy says is the threat, and what he asks the Soviets to do. Ask what he promises to do if they refuse. Ask how he speaks of the risk of nuclear war.

We will not prematurely or unnecessarily risk the costs of worldwide nuclear war in which even the fruits of victory would be ashes in our mouth -- but neither will we shrink from that risk at any time it must be faced.

📌 A NOTE FOR YOU

Notice that Kennedy describes the Soviet action as secret and the American answer as firm but limited. Ask what he leaves open for Khrushchev, and what he leaves closed. Notice also that he speaks of risk in the same breath as resolve. You can return to this page as often as you like.
