PRIMARY SOURCE 2
Nimitz, Report on the Battle of Midway (June 1942)
The Pacific commander sums up what happened in the battle and why.
💡 BEFORE YOU READ

After every major battle the navy required its commanders to file a written report. Admiral Nimitz sent his report on Midway in the last days of June 1942. It drew on the reports of the ship captains and squadron leaders, and it was meant for the Navy Department and for the later record. It was secret at the time.

Naval reports use time and place in a fixed way, and they use numbers with care. Dates are given in the local time of the battle. Ships are named in capital letters. The reports are plain and cautious because they must be checked against many other accounts.

Read this as a historian should. Ask what a commander chooses to stress in a report and what he leaves out. Ask how the writer explains the result, and whether he gives credit to luck, to skill, or to something else. Then compare his account with the lesson.

On the basis of information from several sources, it was known by the end of May that the enemy intended to attack Midway with a large force, including carriers, about the first week of June. Our carriers were therefore placed to the northeast of Midway, where they could strike the enemy’s carriers on their flank when the attack on the island began.

On 4 June our search aircraft sighted the enemy carriers. Torpedo squadrons were the first to attack, and they suffered heavy losses. The dive bombers followed and struck the enemy carriers while their decks were crowded with aircraft. Three enemy carriers were left burning within a few minutes. The fourth was attacked that afternoon and was also left burning.
