TODAY'S FOCUS

On January 30, 1933, Adolf Hitler became Chancellor of Germany. He had never won a majority of the votes in a free election. He did not seize power in a revolt. He was invited in by the men who held power already, and they believed they could control him. This lesson follows his road from an unknown soldier to the Chancellor’s office. It prepares you for this week’s discussion question: could the Weimar Republic have been saved?

AN UNKNOWN CORPORAL

Adolf HitlerAH-dolf HIT-lerAdolf Hitler was the Austrian-born leader of the Nazi Party who became Chancellor of Germany in 1933 and ruled as dictator until 1945. Read more below. was born in 1889 in Braunau, a town in Austria near the German border. He twice failed the entrance test for the art academy in Vienna and spent several years in poverty, living in cheap hostels and selling small paintings. In 1913 he moved to Munich, in Germany. When the war began in 1914 he volunteered for a Bavarian regiment. He served as a messenger on the Western Front, was wounded, and won the Iron Cross for bravery. In October 1918 a gas attack left him temporarily blind. He was in a hospital when he learned that Germany had lost. He chose to believe the legend that traitors at home had caused the defeat.

In 1919 Hitler stayed in the army and was sent to spy on a tiny political group in Munich, the German Workers’ Party. He joined it, and he soon found that he had a gift. He could hold a crowd. The party was renamed the National Socialist German Workers’ Party, which became known as the Nazi Party, and Hitler became its leader. Hitler told his listeners that Germany’s suffering was the work of enemies at home. 👤 HISTORICAL FIGURE
Adolf Hitler
AH-dolf HIT-ler

Adolf Hitler (1889 to 1945) was a gifted speaker, a tireless organizer, and a man with a remarkable memory and a gambler’s nerve. He was also ruthless, vain, and filled with a hatred of Jews and of democracy that he never hid. He believed that nations rose or fell in a struggle of races, and that Germany must conquer land in the east.

Historians who follow Plutarch’s habit of judging a man’s character ask what a leader pursued and how he pursued it. Hitler pursued power and conquest, and he used lies and violence without limit. His rule led to a world war and the murder of millions. He died by suicide in Berlin on April 30, 1945.

THE BEER HALL PUTSCH

In 1923, the year of the hyperinflation and the French occupation of the Ruhr, Hitler thought the republic was ready to fall. On the night of November 8 he and his followers burst into a Munich beer hall where the leader of Bavaria, Gustav von Kahr, was making a speech. Hitler fired a pistol into the ceiling and announced a national revolution. The leaders of Bavaria, held at gunpoint, seemed to agree. They went back on their word as soon as they were free.

The next day Hitler and about two thousand supporters marched into the center of Munich. Police fired on them. Sixteen Nazis and four policemen died, and Hitler fled. This failed PutschPOOCHA German word for a sudden attempt to seize power by force. See the glossary box. might have ended his career. He was arrested and tried for treason. But the judges treated him gently, and he used the courtroom as a stage to make speeches that the newspapers printed across Germany. He was sentenced to five years and served only about nine months.

In prison he wrote the first part of Mein Kampfmine KAHMPFThe title of Hitler’s book. It means ‘My Struggle.’ See the glossary box.. The book set out his ideas: hatred of Jews, hatred of democracy and communism, and the claim that Germany needed more land in the east. Hitler also drew a practical lesson. He could not take power by force against the army and the police. He would have to win it through elections, and then use the law to break the law.

THE LONG WAIT AND THE CRASH

After he was freed, Hitler rebuilt the party. For several years it went nowhere. In the election of 1928 the Nazis won only 2.6 percent of the vote and twelve seats in the Reichstag. In the good years after the hyperinflation, few Germans wanted an angry demagogueDEM-uh-gahgA leader who wins power by stirring up the fears and hatreds of the crowd. See the glossary box., a leader who wins power by stirring up fears and hatreds.

The Depression changed this. By 1932 about six million Germans had no work, and many families went hungry. Voters looked for a party that was not blamed for the crisis. In September 1930 the Nazis won 18 percent of the vote and 107 seats. Hitler promised work, bread, and national pride. He flew from city to city by airplane, spoke through loudspeakers, and filled great halls. The Nazis drew voters from every class, from farmers to clerks to unemployed workers. Meanwhile the SA grew to hundreds of thousands of men and fought street battles with the Communists.

In the presidential election of 1932 Hitler ran against the old field marshal Paul von HindenburgPAUL fon HIN-den-burgPaul von Hindenburg was the aged field marshal and war hero who served as President of Germany and who appointed Hitler Chancellor. Read more below.. Hindenburg won, helped by the votes of people who feared Hitler most. Hitler won about 37 percent in the runoff. In July 1932 the Nazis became the largest party in the Reichstag, with 37 percent of the vote. It was the most they would ever win in a free election.

👤 HISTORICAL FIGURE
Paul von Hindenburg
PAUL fon HIN-den-burg

Paul von Hindenburg (1847 to 1934) was a Prussian officer who won a great victory over the Russians at Tannenberg in 1914. With General Ludendorff he led the German army during the last part of the First World War. He was a monarchist at heart, but when he was elected President in 1925 he took his oath to the republic seriously.

In 1932, at the age of eighty-four, he won a second term. Many Social Democrats voted for him because they saw him as the only man who could stop Hitler. Yet in January 1933, urged on by his advisers, he gave Hitler the chancellorship. He died on August 2, 1934, at the age of eighty-six.

THE BACKSTAIRS BARGAIN

Notice that the Nazi tide had begun to ebb. In November 1932 the Nazis lost about two million votes, and their share fell to 33 percent. The party was short of money, and some of its leaders were discouraged. Yet Hindenburg refused to make Hitler Chancellor, and the Chancellor in December, General Kurt von Schleicher, tried to split the Nazis.

Franz von Papen, a former Chancellor who resented Schleicher, made a plan. He met Hitler secretly in January 1933 and made a deal. Papen and his friends would take office with Hitler, and they believed they could control him. They persuaded Hindenburg, who had called Hitler a “Bohemian corporal,” to agree. On January 30, 1933, Hitler was sworn in as Chancellor of a coalitionkoh-uh-LISH-unA government made up of several parties that agree to govern together. See the glossary box., a government made up of several parties. Only three of the eleven cabinet members were Nazis. Papen is reported to have said, “We have hired him.” That night the SA marched through Berlin in a torchlight parade.

Hitler had gained the highest office without a majority, and he had done so by the front door of the constitution.

COULD THE REPUBLIC HAVE BEEN SAVED?

A student who studies this story finds that nothing in it was fixed in advance. The Nazi vote fell in the last free election. Hindenburg might have refused. Schleicher might have succeeded, or the Social Democrats and Communists might have joined to oppose Hitler. Instead the two parties of the left hated each other more than they feared him. Papen and his friends were sure they could use a man whom they thought a mere speaker. Each of these facts could have gone another way.

But look as well at the habits that made such mistakes likely. The great officers, judges, and civil servants of the old Empire had never come to love the republic, and many were glad to see it weakened. Voters were hungry and angry. Many leaders cared more about their own rivalries than about the common good. A republic is kept alive by citizens and officials who will defend it when it is in danger. In 1933 too few of them were willing.
