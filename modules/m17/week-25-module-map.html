MODULE OVERVIEW

Could the Weimar Republic have been saved?

Hitler became Chancellor in January 1933 without winning a majority in any free election, and within two years he ruled alone, mostly by law. This week follows his road to power and the steps after it, and asks where it might have been stopped.

QUICK CHECK-IN
A group of powerful men think they can use an angry leader and then control him. What should a careful person ask?
WHAT’S AHEAD
1
Lesson 1: From the Beer Hall to the Chancellery. How an unknown corporal became the Chancellor of Germany without winning a majority.
2
Primary Source 1: The Enabling Act (March 24, 1933). The law by which the Reichstag gave Hitler's cabinet the power to make laws.
3
Lesson 2: The Nazi State: the Enabling Act and the Nuremberg Laws. Less than two years, a series of lawful steps, and the end of a republic.
4
Primary Source 2: The Nuremberg Laws (September 15, 1935). Two laws that stripped German Jews of citizenship and many other rights.
WHAT YOU WILL TURN IN
  • Module Introduction survey (ungraded).
  • Primary Source Report: a report in Word on the second lesson and its source, including a question about what might have gone differently.
  • Discussion: Could the Weimar Republic have been saved?
  • Module Review survey (ungraded).
