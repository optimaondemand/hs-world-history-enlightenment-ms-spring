PRIMARY SOURCE 1
The Enabling Act (March 24, 1933)
The law by which the Reichstag gave Hitler's cabinet the power to make laws.
💡 BEFORE YOU READ

The Reichstag passed this law on March 23, 1933, and it took effect the next day. Its official title is the Law to Remedy the Distress of People and Reich. It is short. Read the five articles slowly, because the power it hands over is easy to miss in plain, legal words.

Read it as a historian should. Ask who receives power, what limits are placed on that power, and what the law says about the constitution. Then ask how long the law was meant to last, and what actually happened.

Article 1. In addition to the procedure prescribed by the constitution, laws of the Reich may also be enacted by the Reich government.

Article 2. Laws enacted by the Reich government may deviate from the constitution provided they do not affect the institutions of the Reichstag and the Reichsrat. The rights of the President remain undisturbed.

Article 3. Laws enacted by the Reich government shall be issued by the Chancellor and announced in the Reich Gazette. They shall take effect on the day following the announcement, unless they prescribe a different date.

Article 5. This law takes effect on the day of its promulgation. It expires on April 1, 1937; it expires moreover if the present Reich government is replaced by another.

📌 A NOTE FOR YOU

Notice that the law does not say the word dictator. Look at what Article 2 forbids the government to touch, and then look at what the law lets the government change. Ask who decided when the law had expired. You can return to this page as often as you like.
