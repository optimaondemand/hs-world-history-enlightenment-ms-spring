PRIMARY SOURCE 2
Lenin: The Tasks of the Proletariat in the Present Revolution (the April Theses)
Lenin returns to Russia and sets out a program in ten numbered points.
💡 BEFORE YOU READ

Vladimir Lenin arrived in Petrograd on the night of April 3, 1917. The next day he read these ten points aloud to two meetings of revolutionary leaders. Almost everyone present was shocked. Even many Bolsheviks thought he was demanding too much. Lenin published the points in the party newspaper, Pravda, a few days later.

Russia then used the old calendar, thirteen days behind the Western one, so these dates are Old Style. The Provisional Government that Lenin criticizes was the government of liberal politicians that took power when the tsar fell. The soviets were councils of workers and soldiers.

Some of Lenin’s words come from the language of Marxism. The proletariat means the industrial working class. Bourgeoisie means the property-owning middle class. Annexations means the seizure of territory by force. “Revolutionary defencism” was the idea that Russians should keep fighting Germany in order to defend the revolution, a view held by many socialists in 1917.

Read as a historian should. Ask what Lenin wants to change, whom he is arguing against, and what promises he is making to which groups of Russians.

📜 PRIMARY SOURCE
V. I. Lenin, The Tasks of the Proletariat in the Present Revolution
Published in Pravda, April 7, 1917. English translation by Isaacs Bernard, Lenin's Collected Works, vol. 24 (Moscow: Progress Publishers, 1964), pp. 19 to 26, as hosted by the Marxists Internet Archive. Excerpts from Theses 1, 3, 5, and 8. [...] marks a cut. Dashes in the printed text appear as double hyphens.

1) In our attitude towards the war, which under the new [provisional] government of Lvov and Co. unquestionably remains on Russia’s part a predatory imperialist war owing to the capitalist nature of that government, not the slightest concession to “revolutionary defencism” is permissible.

The class-conscious proletariat can give its consent to a revolutionary war, which would really justify revolutionary defencism, only on condition: (a) that the power pass to the proletariat and the poorest sections of the peasants aligned with the proletariat; (b) that all annexations be renounced in deed and not in word; (c) that a complete break be effected in actual fact with all capitalist interests.

[...]

3) No support for the Provisional Government; the utter falsity of all its promises should be made clear, particularly of those relating to the renunciation of annexations. Exposure in place of the impermissible, illusion-breeding “demand” that this government, a government of capitalists, should cease to be an imperialist government.

[...]

5) Not a parliamentary republic--to return to a parliamentary republic from the Soviets of Workers’ Deputies would be a retrograde step--but a republic of Soviets of Workers’, Agricultural Labourers’ and Peasants’ Deputies throughout the country, from top to bottom.

Abolition of the police, the army and the bureaucracy.

The salaries of all officials, all of whom are elective and displaceable at any time, not to exceed the average wage of a competent worker.

[...]

8) It is not our immediate task to “introduce” socialism, but only to bring social production and the distribution of products at once under the control of the Soviets of Workers’ Deputies.

📌 A NOTE FOR YOU

Lenin wrote in short, numbered points, and he meant each one to be a demand. Notice which points deal with the war, which deal with land and banks, and which deal with the form of the government itself. You can return to this page as often as you like.
