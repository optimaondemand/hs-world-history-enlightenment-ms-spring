PRIMARY SOURCE 2
The Secret Protocol of the Nazi-Soviet Pact (August 23, 1939)
Two dictators secretly agree how to divide eastern Europe.
💡 BEFORE YOU READ

The treaty that Germany and the Soviet Union signed on August 23, 1939, was public. It promised that neither would attack the other. The secret protocol attached to it was not public. It was found among captured German papers after the war, and the Soviet government denied its existence until 1989.

Read the excerpt as a historian should. Ask which countries are named in it, and which of them were consulted. Ask what the two sides promise one another, and what the order of the articles suggests about their priorities. Then ask how the first week of September 1939 looks in light of this text.

📜 PRIMARY SOURCE
Secret Additional Protocol to the German-Soviet Non-Aggression Pact
Moscow, August 23, 1939. English wording follows the version commonly published in translation from the German text. [...] marks a cut. Working placeholder: this excerpt was written from memory for the draft and will be replaced with a verified transcription from a cited edition before release.

On the occasion of the signature of the Non-Aggression Pact between the German Reich and the Union of Soviet Socialist Republics the undersigned plenipotentiaries of each of the two parties discussed in strictly confidential conversations the question of the boundary of their respective spheres of influence in Eastern Europe. These conversations led to the following result:

1. In the event of a territorial and political transformation in the areas belonging to the Baltic States (Finland, Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania), the northern boundary of Lithuania shall represent the boundary of the spheres of influence of Germany and U.S.S.R.

2. In the event of a territorial and political transformation of the areas belonging to the Polish state, the spheres of influence of Germany and the U.S.S.R. shall be bounded approximately by the line of the rivers Narew, Vistula and San. The question of whether the interests of both parties make desirable the maintenance of an independent Polish state and how such a state should be bounded can only be definitely determined in the course of further political developments.

3. With regard to Southeastern Europe attention is called by the Soviet side to its interest in Bessarabia. The German side declares its complete political disinterest in these areas.

4. This protocol shall be treated by both parties as strictly secret.

📌 A NOTE FOR YOU

Notice that the protocol speaks of the Polish state as something whose existence is open to question. Ask what the word “secret” in Article 4 tells you about how each government expected its own people to react.
