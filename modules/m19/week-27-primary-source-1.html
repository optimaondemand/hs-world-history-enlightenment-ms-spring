PRIMARY SOURCE 1
The Munich Agreement and the Anglo-German Declaration (1938)
The agreement that gave the Sudetenland to Germany, and the paper Chamberlain waved at the airport.
💡 BEFORE YOU READ

On September 29 and 30, 1938, the leaders of Germany, Britain, France, and Italy met in Munich and signed an agreement about Czechoslovakia. Czechoslovakia was not asked to the meeting. On the morning of September 30, Chamberlain and Hitler also signed a short declaration, on a sheet of paper that Chamberlain carried home.

Read both texts. Ask what the Agreement requires and what it leaves out. Ask what the Declaration promises, and to whom. Then ask what each text would look like to a Czech reader, and to a British reader, in October 1938.

The Munich Agreement and the Anglo-German Declaration
Munich, September 29 to 30, 1938. English wording follows the version commonly published in translation. [...] marks a cut. Working placeholder: this excerpt was written from memory for the draft and will be replaced with a verified transcription from a cited edition before release.

Munich Agreement. Germany, the United Kingdom, France and Italy, taking into consideration the agreement which has been already reached in principle for the cession to Germany of the Sudeten German territory, have agreed on the following terms and conditions governing the said cession and the measures consequent thereon, and by this agreement they each hold themselves responsible for the steps necessary to secure its fulfilment:

1. The evacuation will begin on 1st October.

2. The United Kingdom, France and Italy agree that the evacuation of the territory shall be completed by the 10th October, without any existing installations having been destroyed, and that the Czechoslovak Government will be held responsible for carrying out the evacuation without damage to the said installations.

[...]

Anglo-German Declaration. We, the German Fuehrer and Chancellor and the British Prime Minister, have had a further meeting today and are agreed in recognizing that the question of Anglo-German relations is of the first importance for the two countries and for Europe.

We regard the agreement signed last night and the Anglo-German Naval Agreement as symbolic of the desire of our two peoples never to go to war with one another again.

We are resolved that the method of consultation shall be the method adopted to deal with any other questions that may concern our two countries, and we are determined to continue our efforts to assure the peace of Europe.

📌 A NOTE FOR YOU

Notice who is named as responsible for the evacuation in the Agreement, and who signed the Declaration. Ask which text creates an obligation and which text only states a hope. You can return to this page as often as you like.
