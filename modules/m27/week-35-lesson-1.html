TODAY'S FOCUS

For almost thirty years the Cold War had seemed a fixed fact. In the 1970s both sides tried to ease it. By 1980 it had grown harsh again, and a new set of events had begun that would end it. This lesson follows the years of detente, the Soviet war in Afghanistan, the rise of Solidarity in Poland, and Reagan’s policy toward Moscow. As you read, begin a list of the pressures on the Soviet system, and mark which came from outside and which from within. You will need it for this week’s discussion: did Reagan win the Cold War, or did the Soviet Union lose it?

DETENTE

The Soviets wanted the agreement because it recognized the borders set after the war. The Soviets thought that they had won, but it gave courage to dissidents, who formed groups in Moscow, Prague, and Warsaw to watch whether their governments kept the promise. Detente did not stop either side from competing. The Soviets backed Communist forces in Angola, Ethiopia, and Yemen. Americans asked why the Soviet buildup went on, and the arms race had not slowed.

👤 HISTORICAL FIGURE
Leonid Brezhnev
LAY-oh-NEED BREZH-nef

Leonid Brezhnev (1906 to 1982) was born in Ukraine and trained as an engineer before rising through the Communist Party. In 1964 he helped to remove Khrushchev and became the head of the party. He ruled for eighteen years, and he put stability first. Few officials lost their jobs, and few reforms were tried.

The Soviet Union grew more powerful abroad under Brezhnev, but it slowed at home. Farms and factories fell behind the West, and the leaders grew old. In his last years Brezhnev was often ill and had to be helped through public ceremonies. When he died in 1982, many of his colleagues were nearly as old as he was.

AFGHANISTAN

In 1978 Communist officers took power in Afghanistan, a poor and mountainous country on the Soviet border, and began to remake it by force. They closed mosques and jailed tribal leaders, and the country rose in revolt. In December 1979 Soviet troops entered Kabul, killed the Afghan leader, and installed another. Brezhnev and a few advisers had decided that they could not allow a Communist neighbor to fall. They thought the war would be short.

It was not. The Soviet army, built to fight a war on the plains of Europe, could hold the cities but not the mountains. 👤 HISTORICAL FIGURE
John Paul II
JON PAWL the SEK-und

Karol Wojtyla (1920 to 2005) grew up in Poland. In the Second World War he worked in a quarry and studied for the priesthood in secret, because the Germans had closed the seminaries. He became a priest, a professor of philosophy, and the archbishop of Krakow, where he dealt with the Communist government for years.

He was elected pope in 1978 and took the name John Paul II. He traveled more widely than any pope before him, and he spoke often about human dignity and religious freedom. In 1981 a gunman shot him in Saint Peter’s Square, and he survived. He died in 2005, and was declared a saint in 2014.

👤 HISTORICAL FIGURE
Lech Walesa
LEK vah-WEN-sah

Lech Walesa (born 1943) was an electrician at the Lenin Shipyard in Gdansk. He had been fired in 1976 for his work with the workers’ cause, and in 1980 he climbed over the shipyard wall to join the strike. He helped to lead it to the Gdansk Agreement. He was a good speaker, blunt and brave, and workers trusted him.

He led Solidarity until the martial law of 1981, when he was interned for eleven months. He won the Nobel Peace Prize in 1983. In 1989 he helped negotiate the end of Communist rule, and in 1990 he was elected president of Poland. He lost the office in 1995.

REAGAN AND THE NEW COLD WAR

Ronald Reagan was elected in November 1980 on a promise to rebuild American strength. ReaganRON-ald RAY-gunRonald Reagan was the American president from 1981 to 1989 who combined a military buildup with talks with the Soviet Union. Read more below. thought that detente had left the United States weaker, and he believed that the Soviet system was fragile. He increased defense spending sharply and ordered new missiles for Europe. In March 1983 he called the Soviet Union an evil empire. A few weeks later he announced the Strategic Defense Initiative, a plan to build a defense against missiles. Critics called it Star Wars, and Soviet leaders feared that it might give America a way to strike first. In September 1983 a Soviet fighter shot down a Korean airliner that had strayed into Soviet air space, and 269 people died.

The tension was real. In November 1983 NATO held a war game that the Soviet leaders, who were old and suspicious, seem to have feared might be a cover for a real attack. Historians still argue about how close this brought the world to danger. Reagan also said that nuclear war could not be won and must never be fought, and that he would meet any Soviet leader who would talk seriously. He had also given aid to the mujahideen and, quietly, to Solidarity. He was helped by the fact that, in the Soviet Union, the leaders died one after another. Brezhnev died in 1982, and two aging successors died in quick turn. In March 1985 the Politburo chose a man of fifty-four.

👤 HISTORICAL FIGURE
Ronald Reagan
RON-ald RAY-gun

Ronald Reagan (1911 to 2004) was born in Illinois and worked as a radio announcer and film actor. He led the actors’ union, and in the 1950s he turned from the Democratic to the Republican Party. He served as governor of California from 1967 to 1975, and he won the presidency in 1980.

He was a plain speaker with a gift for putting large ideas into simple words. He believed that communism was doomed by its own faults, and he was willing to say so openly. He also believed in talking with his enemies. He left office in 1989 and in 1994 announced that he had Alzheimer’s disease.
