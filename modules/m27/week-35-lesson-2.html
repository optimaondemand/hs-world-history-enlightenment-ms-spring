TODAY'S FOCUS

In 1985 the Soviet Union had the largest army in the world and thousands of nuclear weapons. In 1991 it was gone. This lesson asks how an empire of that size could vanish in six years, without a great war. Follow three stories: the reforms of Mikhail Gorbachev, the revolutions of 1989 in Eastern Europe, and the final collapse in 1991. As you read, add to your list of pressures, and ask where a different choice would have changed the outcome.

Glasnost, which was meant to help the Party, now showed its crimes and failures. The old Party leaders resisted change, and the reformers wanted more. 👤 HISTORICAL FIGURE
Mikhail Gorbachev
mih-kah-EEL GOR-buh-chof

Mikhail Gorbachev (1931 to 2022) was born into a farming family in the Stavropol region. He worked on a combine harvester as a teenager, studied law in Moscow, and rose through the Communist Party. He became general secretary in 1985, at fifty-four, the youngest man to hold the post in decades.

He wanted to reform the system, not abolish it, and he refused to use force to hold it together. He won the Nobel Peace Prize in 1990, and he was admired in the West and blamed at home for the collapse. He resigned in December 1991. He later said that he had acted in good faith, and that he would do most of it again.

🔧 TECHNOLOGY
The Chernobyl Reactor

The reactor at Chernobyl was of a Soviet design that used graphite to slow the nuclear reaction and water to cool it. The design had a serious fault: under some conditions, the reaction could speed up instead of slow down. On the night of April 25 and 26, 1986, operators ran a test with many safety systems switched off. The reactor surged, and two explosions blew off its heavy lid.

Fire burned for days and sent a cloud of radioactive material across Europe. About 350,000 people were later moved from the area. The number of deaths from the disaster is still debated, because the effects of radiation appear slowly. The Soviet government first said nothing, and Swedish monitors detected the fallout and raised the alarm.

In August Poland had its first non-Communist prime minister in forty years. In Hungary the government dismantled the barbed wire along its border with Austria. Thousands of East Germans, who could now travel to Hungary, crossed into the West. Others filled the embassies in Prague and Warsaw.

In East Germany the regime tried to hold on. In Leipzig, the weekly marches grew from thousands to tens of thousands. On October 9 the police were ready to fire on seventy thousand marchers, and they did not. Soviet troops stayed in their barracks. On November 9, a spokesman for the government, who had misread his notes, said at a press conference that the new travel rules took effect “immediately.” Crowds poured to the Berlin Wall that night, and the guards, who had no orders, opened the gates. Within weeks Czechoslovakia’s Velvet Revolution brought the playwright Vaclav Havel to the presidency. In Romania the dictator Ceausescu tried to use force, and was tried and shot on Christmas Day.

Notice that there was one place where the story went differently. In June 1989 the Communist rulers of China sent tanks into Tiananmen Square, killed hundreds or more of the students, and kept power. Their leaders did what Gorbachev would not do. In Eastern Europe the decision not to use force was the hinge. 1991: THE END OF THE SOVIET UNION

Inside the Soviet Union, the republics wanted out. The Baltic nations, Estonia, Latvia, and Lithuania, which Stalin had seized in 1940, led the way. Lithuania declared independence in March 1990. In the Russian republic, Boris Yeltsinbuh-REES YELT-sinBoris Yeltsin was the Russian leader who stood against the coup of 1991 and became the first president of the new Russia. Read more below. was elected president in June 1991. He stood against the Party, and he became more popular than Gorbachev.

In August 1991, hardliners in the government, afraid that Gorbachev was about to sign a treaty giving the republics more power, tried a coupKOOA sudden seizing of power from a government, often by a small group using force. See the glossary box.. They put him under guard at his vacation house in the Crimea and sent tanks into Moscow. Yeltsin stood on a tank in front of the Russian parliament and called on the people to resist. Thousands gathered. The army would not fire on them, and the coup fell apart in three days. Gorbachev came back, but the power was gone. The Soviet economy was falling apart, with prices rising and people queuing for bread. The Communist Party was banned, and the republics declared independence one after another. In December, Ukraine voted by a large majority to leave.

On December 8 the leaders of Russia, Ukraine, and Belarus met in a forest lodge and announced that the Soviet Union no longer existed. On December 25, 1991, Gorbachev resigned on television, and the red flag came down from the Kremlin that evening. The empire of Lenin and Stalin ended without a battle, though violence broke out later in several of the new states. You will read part of Gorbachev’s farewell this week.

👤 HISTORICAL FIGURE
Boris Yeltsin
buh-REES YELT-sin

Boris Yeltsin (1931 to 2007) was a construction engineer who rose in the Party and became its chief in Moscow. Gorbachev promoted him, and then drove him out in 1987 when he criticized the pace of reform. He came back in 1989 by winning a seat in the new parliament by a very large vote.

As president of Russia he led the resistance to the 1991 coup, and he helped to bring about the end of the Soviet Union. His own years as president were hard, with a sharp fall in living standards, a war in Chechnya, and many charges of corruption. He resigned on December 31, 1999.

FOR YOUR DISCUSSION POST

Did Reagan win the Cold War, or did the Soviet Union lose it? Review your list. Place a mark by each pressure that came from outside, such as the arms buildup, aid to Afghan rebels, and the Pope. Place another by each that came from within: the failing economy, the Soviet leaders’ loss of nerve, Gorbachev’s choices, and the peoples who wanted to be free. Ask which would have mattered if the others had been absent, and what would have happened if the men in Moscow had used force, as the men in Beijing did. Then write your answer.
