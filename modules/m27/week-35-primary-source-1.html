PRIMARY SOURCE 1
Reagan, Address at the Brandenburg Gate (June 12, 1987)
The American president speaks beside the Berlin Wall and addresses the Soviet leader.
💡 BEFORE YOU READ

President Reagan spoke at the Berlin Wall on June 12, 1987, the 750th anniversary of the founding of the city. He stood in front of the Brandenburg Gate, which lay just behind the wall on the eastern side, in the shadow of the line of concrete and wire. His advisers had argued about the speech. Some at the State Department thought one of its lines was too provocative and tried to remove it.

A few words may be new. “Liberalization” means making a government less strict. “Barriers” means walls or fences. “Prosperity” means wealth and well-being. “Unmistakable” means impossible to mistake.

Read this as a historian should. Ask what Reagan says about the connection between freedom and peace. Ask what he asks Gorbachev to do, and what he offers him in return. Ask what choice the speech leaves to the Soviet leader.

We welcome change and openness; for we believe that freedom and security go together, that the advance of human liberty can only strengthen the cause of world peace.

There is one sign the Soviets can make that would be unmistakable, that would advance dramatically the cause of freedom and peace.

General Secretary Gorbachev, if you seek peace, if you seek prosperity for the Soviet Union and Eastern Europe, if you seek liberalization: Come here to this gate! Mr. Gorbachev, open this gate! Mr. Gorbachev, tear down this wall!

📌 A NOTE FOR YOU

Notice that the speech welcomes some of what Gorbachev has done and then asks for a clear act. Ask whether Reagan is threatening Gorbachev or offering him a way forward, and why he chose to make the request in public. Remember that the wall stood for two more years.
