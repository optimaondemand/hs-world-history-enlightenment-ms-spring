Gorbachev, Resignation Address (December 25, 1991)
The last Soviet leader ends the Soviet Union in a few minutes on television.
💡 BEFORE YOU READ

Mikhail Gorbachev gave this address on Soviet television on the evening of December 25, 1991. Three weeks earlier the leaders of Russia, Ukraine, and Belarus had announced that the Soviet Union no longer existed. Gorbachev had refused to accept this for some time. His speech was short, and it was the last official act of a state that had lasted seventy-four years.

The text comes to us in an English translation. A few words may be new. “Apprehension” means worry about what may come. “Totalitarian” refers to a system in which the state controls all of life. “Dismantled” means taken apart. “Compatriots” means people of the same country.

Read this as a historian should. Ask what Gorbachev says he has achieved. Ask what he says about the old system and what he says about the new. Ask what tone he takes toward the people who are listening.

📌 A NOTE FOR YOU

Notice what Gorbachev counts as his achievements and what he admits was left unfinished. Ask whether he takes the blame for the collapse, and whether he says that it could have gone differently. Notice also the last line you have just read. You can return to this page as often as you like.
