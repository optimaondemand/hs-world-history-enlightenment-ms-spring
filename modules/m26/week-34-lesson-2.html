PALESTINE AND THE FOUNDING OF ISRAEL

The land called Palestine had been part of the Ottoman Empire. Jewish immigration grew, especially after Hitler came to power, and Arab and Jewish communities fought each other and the British. After the Holocaust, thousands of survivors tried to reach Palestine, and Britain, unable to keep order, handed the problem to the United Nations.

No Arab state in the plan came into being.

👤 HISTORICAL FIGURE
David Ben-Gurion
DAY-vid ben-GOOR-ee-un

David Ben-Gurion (1886 to 1973) was born in Poland and went to Palestine at nineteen. He became the chief organizer of the Jewish community there, and in the 1930s and 1940s he led its political and military institutions. In the war of 1948 he made the hard decisions, among them the declaration itself, which some of his colleagues thought too risky.

As prime minister he built the army, the civil service, and a system of settlement and immigration. He was stubborn, blunt, and impatient with rivals. He retired to a desert settlement in 1953 and returned to office two years later. He resigned for good in 1963.

Nasser wanted money to build a huge dam on the Nile at Aswan. The United States and Britain offered to help, then withdrew the offer in July 1956. A week later Nasser answered by announcing that Egypt would nationalizeNASH-uh-nul-eyezTo take a private business or industry under the control of the government. About two-thirds of Europe’s oil passed through the canalThe Suez CanalThe Suez Canal is a waterway across Egypt that joins the Mediterranean and the Red Sea and shortens the sea route between Europe and Asia. Read more below.. Britain’s prime minister, Anthony Eden, compared Nasser to Mussolini, and he and the French decided to remove him.

In late October 1956 the two powers met secretly with Israel at Sèvres, near Paris. The plan was that Israel would attack across the Sinai, and that Britain and France would then demand that both armies withdraw, an ultimatum that Egypt would refuse. They would then land troops to protect the canal. It worked as planned until the landing. Israel attacked on October 29, and the ultimatum was sent the next day. But the American president, Eisenhower, was furious that he had not been told, and he would not accept an invasion of a nation of the Third World by his allies. At the same time, Soviet tanks were crushing the Hungarian revolt, and Eisenhower did not want to seem to excuse it. The United States let the British pound fall by refusing to help it. Britain and France were forced to stop on November 6, before they had taken the whole canal.

Suez was a defeat for Britain and France, and the end of their claim to be great powers. Eden resigned. Nasser, though his army had been beaten in the field, was a hero to the Arab world, because he had stood up to two empires and won. The lesson that Moscow and Washington drew was that the old powers could no longer act alone. It also showed how swiftly a quarrel at the edge of empire could involve both superpowers.

👤 HISTORICAL FIGURE
Gamal Abdel Nasser
guh-MAHL AB-dul NAH-ser

Gamal Abdel Nasser (1918 to 1970) was the son of a postal clerk and became an army officer. He was wounded in the war of 1948 against Israel, and he came home angry at the weak and corrupt government that had sent the army to fight. In 1952 he helped to plan the officers’ coup that drove out the king.

As president he drew power to himself, outlawed rival parties, and jailed his opponents. After Suez, his radio speeches carried across the Arab world. His defeat in the Six-Day War of 1967 broke his reputation. He died of a heart attack in 1970, and millions mourned in the streets of Cairo.

🔧 TECHNOLOGY
The Suez Canal

The canal was built by a French company under Ferdinand de Lesseps and opened in 1869. It runs about a hundred miles through the desert, with no locks, because the two seas are at nearly the same level. Thousands of Egyptian laborers dug it, and many died.

The canal cut the voyage from Britain to India by about four thousand miles, because ships no longer had to sail around Africa. Britain bought a large share in 1875 and later used it to justify keeping troops in Egypt. In 1956 about a third of all the oil that reached Europe came through it, which is why the nationalization caused such alarm.

THE LATER WARS AND AN UNEASY PEACE

In May 1967 Nasser sent troops into the Sinai, ordered out the United Nations force, and closed the Straits of Tiran to Israeli ships. On June 5, Israel struck first and destroyed most of the Egyptian air force on the ground. In six days it took the Sinai and Gaza from Egypt, the West Bank and East Jerusalem from Jordan, and the Golan Heights from Syria. The United Nations then asked Israel to withdraw from occupied territory and asked its neighbors to accept its right to exist. The wording was vague, and each side read it its own way. In October 1973, on the Jewish holy day of Yom Kippur, Egypt and Syria attacked by surprise. Israel was nearly overrun, then recovered, helped by an American airlift of arms.

President Anwar Sadat of Egypt drew a surprising lesson from the war. In 1977 he flew to Jerusalem and spoke to the Israeli parliament. The next year, with the help of President Carter at Camp David, he and Israel’s prime minister Menachem Begin agreed on a peace treaty, signed in 1979. Israel returned the Sinai. Sadat was murdered by Muslim extremists in 1981. The treaty has held, but the dispute over the land and the people of Palestine has not been settled.
