George W. Bush, Address to a Joint Session of Congress (September 20, 2001)
The president tells Congress and the nation how America will answer the attacks.
💡 BEFORE YOU READ

President George W. Bush spoke to a joint session of Congress and to the nation nine days after the attacks of September 11, 2001. The smoke was still rising from the site of the World Trade Center. About eighty million Americans watched. He was speaking to the families of the dead, to the Congress, to the allies of the United States, and to those who had planned the attacks.

A few words may be new. “Resolution” means firm purpose. “Global reach” means able to act in many parts of the world. “Terrorist” means one who uses violence against ordinary people to spread fear. “Assemble” means to gather together.

Read this as a historian should. Ask how Bush describes the attackers and what he says they hate. Ask how wide he says the war will be, and when he says it will end. Ask what he asks of other nations.

📌 A NOTE FOR YOU

Notice that the speech defines the enemy by what it hates and sets no limit of place or time on the war. Ask what that wide aim makes possible, and what it might cost. Ask whether the last line you have just read invites other nations in or pushes them away. You can return to this page as often as you like.
