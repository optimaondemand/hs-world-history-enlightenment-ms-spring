PRIMARY SOURCE 1
Mandela, Statement from the Dock at the Rivonia Trial (April 20, 1964)
A man on trial for his life tells the court what he fought for.
💡 BEFORE YOU READ

Nelson Mandela made this statement from the dock at the Rivonia trial in Pretoria on April 20, 1964. He and nine other men were charged with sabotage and with plotting to overthrow the government. His lawyers knew that he might be sentenced to death. He spoke for about four hours, without being interrupted. The judge sentenced him to life in prison instead.

A few words may be new. “Domination” means rule over others. “Harmony” means peaceful agreement. “Disabilities” means the legal limits placed on a group. “Ideal” means a goal that one holds up to guide one’s life.

Read this as a historian should. Ask what Mandela says he fought against, and what he says he fought for. Ask how he describes the ideal he hopes to reach. Ask what he says he is prepared to do for it.

I have fought against white domination, and I have fought against black domination. I have cherished the ideal of a democratic and free society in which all persons live together in harmony and with equal opportunities. It is an ideal which I hope to live for and to achieve. But if needs be, it is an ideal for which I am prepared to die.

📌 A NOTE FOR YOU

Notice that Mandela denies that he fights for black rule. Ask what he is fighting for instead, and why a man in his position would say it. Ask whether the closing words are a threat, a hope, or both. You can return to this page as often as you like.
