PRIMARY SOURCE 1
The Lytton Report (1932)
An international commission judges Japan's conquest of Manchuria.
💡 BEFORE YOU READ

After the Mukden Incident, the League of Nations sent a commission to Manchuria under Lord Lytton, a British statesman. Its members came from Britain, France, Germany, Italy, and the United States. They traveled through China and Manchuria for months and published their report in October 1932.

The report was careful and balanced. It did not simply side with China. It admitted that Japan had real grievances and real interests in Manchuria. But on the key facts it ruled against Japan. Read the excerpt and ask what the commission says about the attack, about Manchukuo, and about what the League should do. Then ask what the League could do to enforce it.

📌 A NOTE FOR YOU

Notice that the commission does not say Japan has no interests in Manchuria. Ask what the report says about the old situation, and what it says of the new one. Then ask what power stood behind these words. You can return to this page as often as you like.
