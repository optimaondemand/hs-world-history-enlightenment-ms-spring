MODULE OVERVIEW

What did the Allies know, and what could they have done?

In 1942 and 1943 the tide of the war turned, from the Volga to Egypt, and in 1944 to Normandy and the Ardennes. The same armies were closing in on a crime that was being carried out in secret. This week asks you to study that crime slowly and with respect, and to keep apart what governments knew, what choices they had, and what they decided.

QUICK CHECK-IN
Some people helped their neighbors at great risk, and some did not. What do you think made the difference?
WHAT’S AHEAD
1
Lesson 1: Stalingrad, El Alamein, D-Day, and the Bulge. How the Allies turned the tide against Germany between 1942 and 1945.
2
Primary Source 1: Eisenhower, Order of the Day and the Unused Note (June 1944). The Supreme Commander writes to his troops, and writes a statement he hoped never to use.
3
Lesson 2: The Holocaust: Wannsee and What the Allies Knew. The murder of the Jews of Europe, and the hard questions it leaves about what the free world knew and did.
4
Primary Source 2: The Wannsee Protocol (January 20, 1942). The minutes of a meeting where German officials planned the murder of Europe’s Jews.
WHAT YOU WILL TURN IN
  • Module Introduction survey (ungraded).
  • Primary Source Report: a report in Word on the second lesson and its source, including a question about what might have gone differently.
  • Discussion: What did the Allies know, and what could they have done?
  • Module Review survey (ungraded).
START HERE

Begin with the Module Introduction survey (22.1), then open 22.3, Lesson 1: Stalingrad, El Alamein, D-Day, and the Bulge.
