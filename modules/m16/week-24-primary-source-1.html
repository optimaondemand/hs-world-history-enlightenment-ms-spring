PRIMARY SOURCE 1
Lenin, Letter to the Congress (“the Testament”), 1922 to 1923
The dying founder of the Soviet state describes the men who may succeed him.
💡 BEFORE YOU READ

Lenin dictated these notes in December 1922 and January 1923, after his strokes and before his death. They were meant to be read to the next party congress. In them he looks at the leading men of the party and asks what dangers the party faces. Most of the notes concern the fear of a split between Stalin and Trotsky.

The excerpt below is the part about Stalin, together with the postscript of January 4, 1923. Read it as a historian should. Ask what Lenin fears about Stalin, what he suggests the party do, and why he thinks the matter urgent. Ask also what the party did in the end.

Comrade Stalin, having become General Secretary, has unlimited authority concentrated in his hands, and I am not sure whether he will always be capable of using that authority with sufficient caution. [...] On the other hand, Comrade Trotsky is distinguished not only by his exceptional abilities. Personally he is, to be sure, the most capable man in the present Central Committee, but he has displayed excessive self-assurance.

[...]

Postscript (January 4, 1923). Stalin is too rude, and this fault, entirely supportable in relations among us Communists, becomes insupportable in the office of General Secretary. Therefore I propose to the comrades to find a way to remove Stalin from that position and appoint to it another man who in all other respects differs from Comrade Stalin in having only one advantage, namely, that of being more tolerant, more loyal, more polite and more considerate to the comrades, less capricious, etc.

📌 A NOTE FOR YOU

Notice that Lenin does not accuse Stalin of a crime or a plan. He names a quality of character and a danger of the office. Ask whether a rule or a person would have been the better guard against that danger. You can return to this page as often as you like.
