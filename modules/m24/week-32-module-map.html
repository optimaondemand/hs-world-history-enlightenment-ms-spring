MODULE OVERVIEW

Who divided Europe?

The three allies who beat Germany became rivals in less than two years. This week follows Yalta and Potsdam, the Iron Curtain, the Marshall Plan, the Berlin airlift, and two armed alliances. As you read, keep a chart of Soviet actions, Western actions, and conditions that neither side chose.

QUICK CHECK-IN
A country offers aid to a hungry continent and invites everyone, including its rival. What does that tell you about its aims?
WHAT’S AHEAD
1
Lesson 1: Yalta, Potsdam, Kennan, and the Truman Doctrine. How the wartime allies became rivals, and how the United States chose to answer.
2
Primary Source 1: Kennan, the Long Telegram (February 22, 1946). An American diplomat in Moscow explains how the Soviet leaders think.
3
Lesson 2: The Marshall Plan, the Berlin Blockade, NATO, and the Warsaw Pact. How aid, a blockade, and two alliances turned a quarrel into two armed camps.
4
Primary Source 2: Truman, Address on Greece and Turkey (March 12, 1947). The president asks Congress for aid, and sets out a new American policy.
WHAT YOU WILL TURN IN
  • Module Introduction survey (ungraded).
  • Reading Quiz: seven questions on both lessons and both primary sources.
  • Discussion: Who divided Europe?
  • Module Review survey (ungraded).
START HERE

Begin with the Module Introduction survey (24.1), then open 24.3, Lesson 1: Yalta, Potsdam, Kennan, and the Truman Doctrine.
